1. Overview

MAHJ Learn is an educational app for learning and practicing American Mahjong, a four-player tile-matching pattern game played with an annual league card. The game involves no money, no chips, no wagering, and no gambling of any kind.

This policy describes what data the app and its third-party service providers handle. It distinguishes clearly between what the app itself stores on your device and what third-party SDKs may process.

Affiliation notice: MAHJ Learn is an independent educational app and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by the National Mah Jongg League (NMJL). All trademarks and the annual NMJL card are the property of their respective owners.

2. Data the app itself collects: none

The app has no accounts, developer-operated servers, first-party analytics, crash reporting, or telemetry. Embedded advertising and purchase services may process the data described in Section 3.

  • All of your progress, settings and preferences are stored locally on your device only, using the WebView storage known as localStorage.
  • Your progress never leaves your device. It is not uploaded, synced or backed up anywhere.
  • If you delete the app (or clear its data in your device settings), this local data is permanently deleted. We cannot recover it, because we never receive it.

Audio files used by the app are bundled locally inside the app and do not transmit anything.

3. Third-party services

Although the app itself collects nothing, the following third-party services embedded in the app may process limited data as described. (This section applies once these services are enabled in the version of the app you are using; if a service is not active in your build, it processes no data.) We do not control these services; please review their own privacy policies.

3.1 Google AdMob (advertising) and on-device sharing

The free tier can be paid at its checkpoints with rewarded video ads. A rewarded video ad is a short video advertisement supplied by Google AdMob. The free tier asks for one at a checkpoint: the break between the Charleston and play, the next game of the day, another Hand Spotter round, or the third and later sections of the card. Watching the video earns the unlock; closing it early earns nothing. When ads are requested, Google may process:

  • Device identifiers, including the Android Advertising ID and, on iOS, the Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA). The IDFA is only available if you grant permission via Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt (see Section 4);
  • Approximate location derived from your IP address (used for ad compliance and relevance);
  • Product interactions and advertising data, such as app launches, taps, video views, and ads seen or completed, used for ad delivery, measurement, and analytics;
  • SDK diagnostics, such as crash logs, app launch time, hang rate, energy use, and other technical diagnostic data, used to diagnose and improve the advertising SDK and, where applicable, for advertising and analytics.

Google may use these identifiers to serve and measure ads, including personalized ads where you have given consent (in the EEA, UK, and Switzerland, via the consent form described in Section 4) or where permitted by law. You can reset or delete your Android advertising ID in your device settings (Settings → Google → Ads) and opt out of ads personalization there as well.

Google's privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy
Google (AdMob) advertising-specific disclosures: policies.google.com/technologies/ads

Sharing instead of an ad

At a checkpoint you can also choose Share instead. Sharing is completely optional: it is offered as an alternative to the ad, and it draws on the same daily allowance, so sharing can never give you a longer day than watching ads would. The app draws a 1200×630 puzzle card on your device showing a 13-tile position from the app, then hands that image to your device's system share sheet (or, in a browser, the browser's own share sheet) so you can send it with an app you choose. The image is generated on your device: this share path contains no advertising SDK, no analytics, and no server of ours; nothing is posted by the app, and no data is sent to us.

The app receives no confirmation of what you shared: it never gets a copy of the image, the recipient, or any notice that something was posted. On Android the operating system only reports that the share sheet opened; on iOS it reports which activity you completed, and backing out of the sheet means no unlock is granted. If sharing is impossible on your device (no share sheet available), the app still releases that checkpoint rather than trapping you, using the day's single free unlock that also covers a failed ad.

The Full Version has no ads and no checkpoints, so sharing is never needed after purchase.

3.2 RevenueCat (in-app purchases)

The app's one-time purchase is managed through RevenueCat, which processes purchase and entitlement data (e.g., transaction identifiers and whether you own the app's Full Version) so the app can verify and restore your purchase across devices and reinstalls. RevenueCat acts on behalf of the app developer; Apple or Google processes the actual payment, and we do not receive your payment card details.

RevenueCat's privacy policy: revenuecat.com/privacy

4. Advertising identifiers, consent, and tracking

  • Android: the Google Mobile Ads SDK uses the Android Advertising ID (AAID) to deliver and measure ads. You can reset or delete it in device settings, and the SDK respects your "opt out of ads personalization" setting.
  • iOS: Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) framework governs access to the IDFA. If you decline the ATT prompt (or no prompt is shown), the IDFA is unavailable to the app; contextual ads may still be shown. Where the ATT prompt is used, its purpose is explained in the system dialog.
  • EEA / UK / Switzerland: before ads are served, the app uses Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) consent form to ask for your consent for ads personalization and the use of identifiers/local storage, as required by Google's EU user consent policy. You can change your choice at any time via the "Ad privacy options" entry in the app's settings.

5. Children's privacy

MAHJ Learn is a general-audience game. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13. The app itself has no accounts and no data collection. If you believe a child has provided personal information through one of our third-party services, contact us at mahj_learn@outlook.com and we will address it.

6. Data sharing and sales

  • The app does not sell your personal information.
  • Google processes the limited device, location, interaction, advertising, and diagnostic data described in Section 3.1 to provide and measure ads. RevenueCat processes purchase and entitlement data as described in Section 3.2 to verify and restore the Full Version.
  • If you tap Share instead at a checkpoint, you send the puzzle card yourself through your device's share sheet. Nothing is posted by the app, and we receive no copy of the image, no recipient and no confirmation of that share.
  • We have no access to your on-device game progress. Apple or Google processes the payment; we do not receive your payment card details.

7. Your rights and choices

  • Local data: delete the app or clear its storage to erase all locally stored progress.
  • Ad identifiers: reset/delete the Android advertising ID in device settings; on iOS, decline tracking in the ATT prompt or reset the advertising identifier in Settings → Privacy → Tracking.
  • EEA / UK / Switzerland: you may withdraw or change your ads consent at any time through the in-app "Ad privacy options" entry. You also have GDPR rights (access, erasure, etc.). However, because we store no personal data about you on any server, in most cases there is no data for us to access or delete; requests relating to ad data should be directed to Google per its privacy policy.
  • Purchases: purchase records are handled by Apple/Google and RevenueCat under their respective policies.

8. Security

Local progress data remains on your device under your device's security model. Data transmitted to third-party services (ad requests, purchase verification) is encrypted in transit per those providers' practices.
